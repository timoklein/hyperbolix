    Changelog¶

    All notable changes to Hyperbolix will be documented in this file.

    The format is based on Keep a Changelog, and this project adheres to Semantic Versioning.

    Unreleased¶

    Fixed¶

    • The origin Jacobian of Hyperboloid.expmap / expmap_0 was √c·I instead of I for every c < 1. It bit whenever the tangent vector satisfied ‖v‖ ≲ 1e-15/√c, the zero tangent included: the divisor of the sinh(t)/t ratio carried a second MIN_NORM floor (denom = floor_at(√c·‖v‖, MIN_NORM)) that the numerator did not, so at v = 0 the ratio evaluated to √c·1e-15 / 1e-15 = √c rather than 1 and the whole spatial Jacobian came out scaled by √c — a zero-initialised gyro-bias saw its first gradient scaled by 0.32 at c = 0.1, 0.55 at c = 0.3, in both dtypes (c ≥ 1 was unaffected, since the floor is inactive there). Found by an independent numerics review. The divisor is now the same floored t = √c·‖v‖, which is ≥ √c·1e-15 > 0 and normal in both dtypes, so sinh(t)/t is exactly 1 there; the Poincaré, stereographic and proper-velocity exponential maps already divided by a single floored quantity and are unchanged. test_hyperboloid_expmap_at_the_origin_has_the_identity_jacobian pins the identity at c ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1, 2.5} in both dtypes, and test_hyperboloid_gyro_bias_at_the_origin_has_a_nonzero_jacobian now runs at c = 0.1 as well as c = 1.
    • The norm-rescale divisor was not an exact power of two in float64 on CUDA, so the rescale it exists to make rounding-free put a rounding into every division it scales. _pow2_divisor built the divisor with jnp.ldexp, which computes m · 2**e and lowers to an XLA power — a ~1 ulp transcendental on CUDA rather than an exact exponent shift. On an A100 (jax 0.9.1) that came out an ulp low for 75 of the 600 binades k ∈ [-300, 300); ldexp(1.0, -33) returns one ulp below 2**-33. The divisor is now built by masking the mantissa bits off m, which leaves 2**floor(log2 m) exactly on every backend, with the old minexp floor replaced by a finfo.tiny floor (a subnormal m masks to 0). Affected safe_norm, safe_hypot, safe_hypot_norm and safe_normalize on GPU, in float64 only — float32 was already exact on both backends, and CPU float64 was too; values there are bit-identical before and after (checked over every binade of both dtypes plus 0, inf, nan, the subnormals, finfo.tiny and finfo.max). It showed up as test_the_norm_rescale_adds_no_rounding_of_its_own failing on GPU with mean ulp distance 0.27–0.34 against 0.000 on CPU. test_the_rescale_divisor_is_an_exact_power_of_two_on_every_backend now pins zero mantissa bits and the correct binade over the full exponent range of both dtypes.

    Changed¶

    • Float32 matmul precision is now split by what the dot product computes, not by where it lives, and there is no hyperbolix-specific knob for it. MATMUL_PRECISION = jax.lax.Precision.HIGHEST is pinned on the geometry and is not user-configurable: the vector-vector reductions in manifolds/ (behind dist, addition, gyration, expmap, logmap, ptransp, tangent_inner, egrad2rgrad, the Lorentz inner product, the Busemann coordinate and the boundary factors) and the HoroPCA contractions in decomposition/; lorentz_midpoint and the Poincaré poincare_weighted_midpoint, the two Fréchet-mean normalizers whose cancellation-free identity only holds to O(eps) on float32-accurate inputs; the conv_general_dilated_patches patch extraction in the hyperboloid, Poincaré and proper-velocity convolutions, which XLA implements as a convolution against a 0/1 filter — a pure data copy, where TF32 would round every input to a 10-bit mantissa for no throughput at all; the attention score and aggregation einsums in hyperboloid_attention.py (the Lorentzian similarity, the softmax scores and weighted values, the linear-attention kernel sums) and the spatial residual projection added to that aggregate; and the point-to-hyperplane einsums — HypRegressionHyperboloid and FGGLorentzMLR plus the three batched _compute_mlr einsums in manifolds/hyperboloid.py, poincare.py and proper_velocity.py — whose asinh argument is a difference of a radial and an angular term that cancels. That last pin reaches into the hidden layers too: HypLinearHyperboloidPLFC, HypLinearPoincarePP, HypLinearPV, HypConv2DHyperboloidILNN, HypConv2DPoincare and HypConv2DPV are those einsums applied to their own kernel, so their weight GEMM runs at HIGHEST as well. What the head's pin costs, measured on an H100 against 1.1.2 (every dot at TF32): the HypRegressionHyperboloid forward alone, B=1e7, 10 classes, is 2.6x at D=128 and 2.0x at D=32 (1.02x of 1.2.0, which pinned it too); in a training step expmap_0 → HTCLinear ×2 → HypRegressionHyperboloid at B=262144, D=128 the pin is the residual 1.05x over 1.1.2 once the GEMM pin and the norms are given back (the same step was 1.00x on the tree before the split). The hyperbolic attention layers, whose score and aggregation einsums are pinned, are 1.25–1.31x of 1.1.2 at (8, 256, 257) and 0.76–0.80x of 1.2.0. On an A100 (jax 0.9.1) the float32-vs-float64 relative error of lorentz_midpoint is 4.6e-5 … 2.6e-4 at the TF32 default against 2.6e-8 … 1.6e-7 under HIGHEST, a ~2000x difference; the eager float32 gradient of the hyperboloid MLR goes from 1.5e-4 to 2.7e-7 relative over the same switch.
    • The layer weight GEMMs carry no precision keyword at all and follow JAX's own jax_default_matmul_precision — TF32 on Ampere/Hopper. HTCLinear — the attention Q/K/V projections included — FGGLinear/FGGConv2D, the FHCNN and FHNN linear forwards, HypLinearPoincare, HypVQMLRPoincare's implicit-codebook matmul and hybrid_regularization's nnx.Linear. One of them carries a cancellation and is still left on the default: the FGG hidden dot x @ V absorbs the Minkowski metric into V, so the -x0*V0 + <x_s, V_s> cancellation happens inside one accumulation — the same product FGGLorentzMLR pins — and the comment on the jnp.matmul in nn_layers/hyperboloid_linear._fgg_linear_forward says so. It is left there deliberately: at depth 16, c = 1, input geodesic radius 5, an FGGLinear stack's float32 parameter-gradient error under the default is 0.199 % relative L2 against a float64 reference and its input-gradient error 0.274 %, against 1.539 % / 2.013 % for an HTCLinear stack, which has no cancellation at all — so the in-accumulation cancellation is not what dominates there (independent A100 measurement, jax 0.9.1, logs/2026-09-06_numerics_independent_review/training/gpu/depth.json; under global HIGHEST the same four cells are 8.0e-7 / 1.0e-6 and 2.5e-6 / 1.2e-5). Those depth-16 numbers are the worst cell of a 24-cell grid (depths 2/8/16, c ∈ {0.1, 1}, input radius {0.5, 5}), one initialisation and one input draw per cell and no seeds — grid maxima at n = 1, not means, and the HTC stack's measured radius climb (≈0.35 nats per layer at large radius) and the hypothesis linking it to the depth-16 error are in the hyperbolix/utils/precision.py docstring. At depth 2 the choice is invisible in training: 5 seeds per arm, D = 128, B = 256, c = 1, 2 000 Adam updates, mean heldout loss 0.27736 (default) against 0.27738 (HIGHEST) for HTC and 0.28610 against 0.28614 for FGG, on a seed spread of ~0.015–0.017. HTCLinear is where that choice is visible in the test suite: test_attn_output_shape_and_jit and test_causal_first_token_self_only compare two lowerings of the same algebra at a 1e-5 tolerance, which TF32's run-to-run rounding exceeds, so both now wrap their forward passes in jax.default_matmul_precision("highest") — the tolerances are unchanged and the TF32 values are not wrong. These are the sites where HIGHEST actually costs throughput — it replaces one TF32 pass with a three-pass float32 emulation, measured +5.5 % on a jitted hyperbolic attention forward — so the trade is the user's to make, through JAX rather than through a hyperbolix-specific attribute: jax.config.update("jax_default_matmul_precision", "highest"), or scoped, with jax.default_matmul_precision("highest"):. Both are jit-cache aware (changing them re-traces), and both cover the pinned sites too, so one line runs the whole package in full float32. A deep, fully hyperbolic stack is a good reason to set it: a TF32 error introduced in an early layer's weight GEMM is carried by every layer after it, and it is not small. Measured on an A100 (jax 0.9.1, ambient dim 33, batch 64, c in {0.1, 1.0} x spatial radius in {0.5, 5, 20}, float32 against a float64 reference): FGGLinear 2.6e-4 at the TF32 default against 6.8e-8 under HIGHEST, FGGLorentzMLR 1.3e-4 … 3.6e-4 against 3.6e-8 … 9.6e-8 — roughly ~1e-4 against ~1e-7 on the layers, three of float32's seven significant digits. HIGHEST is a no-op on CPU (no TF32 path) and for float64 anywhere.
    • The per-sample norms that 1.2.0 measurably made slower are a single reduction again — and only those. A site is converted where 1.2.0 measured slower (hyperboloid expmap_0 1.28x, HTCLinear forward+backward 1.12x, on both A100 and H100) or where PR #75 raised the compiled kernel / reduction count. That is: Hyperboloid.proj/proj_batch/dist_0 (both version slots); the shared gyrovector proj/proj_batch behind Poincare and Stereographic, and Poincare.expmap; and spatial_to_hyperboloid (so HTCLinear), the FHCNN linear forward and the FGG linear forward. Everywhere else 1.2.0's max-scaled two-pass norm stays, with its full-range guarantee. In isolation that costs something — about 1.2–1.35x the one-pass form on a forward pass in float32 (h_logmap_0 and Poincare.expmap_0 at D = 32/B = 4096 and D = 128/B = 262144, c ∈ {0.1, 1}: 1.202–1.343, independent A100 measurement, jax 0.9.1, logs/2026-09-06_numerics_independent_review/timing/norm/ — this range rests on that measurement alone; the 1.28x above is a v1.2.0-vs-v1.1.2 tree ratio of a converted site, not a one-pass/two-pass swap of a retained one) — but inside a training step it shrinks to ≤ ~1.05x (0.92–1.05x on the same probe's complete-step and HTC-chart rows), which is why the two-pass norm is kept wherever the operand's magnitude is unbounded. The retained sites: hyperboloid logmap_0 (which measured faster in 1.2.0), the pairwise dist/logmap polar frame, hyperboloid and Poincaré tangent_norm, Poincare.expmap_0 (no measured change either way), the FHNN linear forward, the linear-attention focus_transform (converting it measured no speedup of its own), every ProperVelocity operation and the proper-velocity isometry maps, the Poincaré metric-tensor distances, the rest of Stereographic, Euclidean and ProductManifold, and the wrapped-normal log_prob. The converted spellings are safe_sqrt(sum(v**2)) where the input can be exactly zero, a plain sqrt(const + sum(v**2)) where a strictly positive constant is added, and floor_at(..., MIN_NORM) around either where the norm is a divisor. Every gradient fix from 1.2.0 is kept: safe_sqrt's double-where gives the same exact 0 and exactly-zero VJP at the zero vector that safe_norm did, so jax.grad at coincident points and at the origin stays finite.
    • Past float32 coordinate 1.8e19 the converted sites give up their answer, and what they give instead was measured rather than argued. That coordinate is geodesic radius ~45 at c = 1 and ~139 at c = 0.1, in float32; a network out there is already diverging, and inf is the intended signal rather than a plausible finite answer. Measured, float32, c = 1: Hyperboloid.proj/proj_batch give x0 = inf with the spatial part unchanged and no NaN; dist_0 gives inf on both version slots; spatial_to_hyperboloid/HTCLinear and the FHCNN forward give x0 = inf with no NaN; the FGG linear forward gives a non-finite output (NaN or inf, depending on the input — an all-NaN output on the probe's 5e19 spatial input, inf with no NaN on a second, crafted one); the Poincaré and κ-stereographic boundary clamps give the origin — a finite point, with a finite (exactly zero) gradient there — because x * (max_norm / inf) is the zero vector, the pre-1.2.0 behaviour; and Poincare.expmap gives the base point, a finite point, inheriting the same clamp. Two of the converted sites therefore do saturate to a finite point, and that is the clamp's documented behaviour. Everything on the two-pass norm — Poincare.expmap_0, which still returns the correct boundary point, and the linear-attention focus_transform included — behaves exactly as in 1.2.0.
    • The maximum radius the converted origin-chart operations can represent drops with it. For proj and dist_0 the ceiling used to be x₀'s — the two-pass norm never formed ‖x_s‖², so what bound the radius was cosh(a)/√c ≤ finfo.max, i.e. a = ln(2·finfo.max): radius ~89 in float32 / ~710 in float64 at c = 1 (89.416 / 710.476, the same number as arcsinh(finfo.max), since sinh(a) and cosh(a) leave the dtype together). It is now the coordinate limit sinh(a)/√c < sqrt(finfo.max) — radius ~45 in float32 and ~356 in float64. logmap_0 and the pairwise dist/logmap keep the two-pass norm and so keep a ceiling of that order: logmap_0 reads only ‖y_s‖ and keeps the old ~89 / ~710 exactly, while the pairwise pair binds a fraction of a radius earlier at e^a/√c ≤ finfo.max (a = ln(finfo.max), 88.72 / 709.78), because _polar_frame forms u = x₀ + ‖x_s‖ and returns ±inf once that overflows. Every hyperbolic model in the literature lives four to five orders of magnitude inside every one of these limits.
    • safe_norm, safe_hypot_norm and safe_normalize remain public and are what every unconverted per-sample site and every once-per-forward weight norm still use. safe_hypot is a hot-path two-leg scalar composer, not a weight-norm primitive: the hyperboloid polar frame builds its radial and angular legs with it.
    • One deliberate value change: the hyperboloid time slot is now sqrt(1/c + sum(x_s**2)) instead of sqrt((1/sqrt(c))**2 + sum(x_s**2)), one rounding fewer and in the more accurate direction (Hyperboloid.proj/proj_batch, nn_layers.hyperboloid_core.spatial_to_hyperboloid, and the FHCNN/FGG layer forwards; isometry_mappings.pv_to_hyperboloid keeps 1.2.0's spelling). Everywhere else safe_norm/safe_hypot_norm were bit-identical to sqrt(sum)/sqrt(sum + q**2) on the finite domain, so values move only by XLA association noise (0-2 ulp).
    • The hyperboloid time slot carries a custom_jvp whose only job is the backward's XLA fusion shape. With the one-reduction sqrt(1/c + sum(x_s**2)), XLA:GPU fused the backward's reduction into a full-width Triton kernel and left the cotangent's re-embedding pad as a standalone copy of the whole batch, so jax.grad of proj/expmap_0 at B=1e7, D=128 ran 1.35x/1.76x slower than 1.1.2 on an H100 even though the forward was as fast; v1.1.2 avoided this only through the maximum(…, MIN_NORM) its VJP happened to place between the reduce and the sqrt. The module-private _time_slot rule states the same derivative AD derives (the 1/c cotangent 1/(2·x0) included, so a traced LearnableCurvature is unaffected) and puts a lax.optimization_barrier on the per-row 1/x0, which restores 1.1.2's kernel structure (proj.grad 3/1, expmap_0.grad 7/3 kernels/reductions at D=128). Forward values are bit-identical; jax.jvp/jacfwd keep working. test_hyperboloid_time_slot_custom_jvp_matches_plain_autodiff checks value, gradient, forward-mode tangent and the curvature cotangent against the rule-free spelling. The fusion effect it fixes is shape- and consumer-dependent, so it will not reproduce everywhere: it appeared in float32 on jax 0.9.1 at B ≥ 1e6, at both D = 32 and D = 128 (expmap_0.grad 1.48x of 1.1.2 at B = 1e6 and 1.76x at B = 1e7 for D = 128, and 1.60x at B = 1e7 for D = 32, with proj.grad 1.35x at either width; absent at B = 1e5, 0.98x at D = 128 — logs/2026-09-04_safe_norm_hot_path_revert/RESULTS_31403c2.md, .../vda/timing_D{32,128}/ and .../vda3/timing_D{32,128}/, two different H100 hosts with every ratio against a v1.1.2 arm from the same host and run), and only for a backward that materialises the full (B, n) cotangent, e.g. jax.grad of a sum over the output. With a scalar-loss consumer the pad fuses anyway: an independent A100 measurement of jax.grad(mean(f(x)**2)) at B ≤ 262144, D = 128 put the barrier arm within ~1 % of the rule-free spelling for expmap_0 forward+backward at the large shape (1.003-1.006 at both c = 1 and c = 0.1, against either spelling without the barrier); the proj rows of the same run are noisier (one contaminated round).
    • One extreme-radius test row moved from float32 to float64. It was exercising the retired overflow guard rather than the algorithm, so it follows the guard rather than being loosened; tolerances are unchanged. In tests/test_manifold_oracles.py, test_hyperboloid_dist_and_logmap_to_the_origin_match_the_origin_variants now takes a per-dtype radius grid — float32 stops at 43, float64 keeps 45. What binds is the spatial coordinate sinh(a)/sqrt(c), which passes float32's 1.845e19 at radius 45 for c = 0.3, where the converted dist_0 returns inf by policy; exactly that one cell of the grid crosses the threshold, and 43 is the repo's existing float32 radial ceiling. This narrows the float32 coverage rather than preserving it: the radius-45 cell is no longer exercised in float32 at all, only in float64. The pairwise oracle rows are untouched, because the pairwise dist/logmap keep the two-pass norm.

    Removed¶

    • The smooth_clamp on the asinh argument of every MLR head is gone, and with it the clamping_factor and smoothing_factor parameters of Hyperboloid.compute_mlr, Poincare.compute_mlr_pp, ProperVelocity.compute_mlr and the four _compute_mlr helpers, plus every layer that only carried them to forward: HypRegressionHyperboloid, HypRegressionPoincare, HypRegressionPoincarePP, HypRegressionPV, HypLinearHyperboloidPLFC, HypLinearPoincarePP, HypLinearPV, HypConv2DHyperboloidILNN, HypConv2DPoincare, HypConv2DPV and HypVQMLRPoincare. This is a signature break: drop the two keywords at the call site. asinh is defined on all of R with derivative 1/√(1+x²) ≤ 1, so it needs no guard — the only infinite argument comes from a point that has already overflowed the float32 manifold, and the clamp turned that into a finite, plausible logit and hid the divergence; the logit is now inf and the loss NaN, which is the failure you want to see. The bound was also dtype-dependent (log(2/eps): ±16.6355 in float32 against ±36.7368 in float64), so the two dtypes disagreed by design: measured with HypRegressionHyperboloid at c = 1, geodesic radius 5, default init, batch 256 × 10 classes, the float32 logits sat 9.4e-2 (D = 128, 1.2% of cells clamped) and 1.8e-1 (D = 32, 22.4% clamped) from the float64 ones relative to the largest logit, identical under TF32 and HIGHEST; with the clamp gone the same cells agree to 6.4e-7 / 4.6e-7. Every clamped cell also had an exactly zero input gradient (smooth_clamp's slope past its bound is exp(-50·d)), freezing those points under SGD, and at c = 1 with the shipped init the float32 bound first bit at geodesic radius 4.25 (D = 32) / 4.75 (D = 128) — well inside the range the library documents as float32-reliable. Neither reference clamps this quantity: HNN++ (mil-tokyo/hyperbolic_nn_plusplus) guards only the 1 - c‖x‖² denominator and the weight norms at a flat 1e-15, and HCNN's LorentzMLR (kschwethelm/HyperbolicCV) has no guard at all; the line came from the author's earlier PyTorch code (univie-dm/hyperbolic_math, helpers.py, "Improve the performance by smoothly clamping the input of asinh()") with no recorded rationale. smooth_clamp itself stays in hyperbolix.utils.math_utils and is unchanged
    • The three float32 overflow oracles in tests/test_manifold_oracles.py are retired, since the guard they tested is gone. test_hyperboloid_proj_time_slot_survives_the_float32_overflow becomes test_hyperboloid_proj_time_slot_is_exact_at_an_enormous_radius in float64, and test_poincare_proj_clamps_a_far_point_to_the_boundary_not_to_the_origin moves to float64; both then assert the algorithm rather than the dtype's range. test_hyperboloid_expmap_0_saturates_to_a_consistent_point_not_an_infinite_time_slot cannot move — sinh clips its argument at 0.99*log(finfo.max) in both dtypes, so the capped spatial value squared overflows in float64 too — and is replaced by test_hyperboloid_expmap_0_past_the_sinh_cap_is_infinite_not_nan, which pins the weaker but real contract: x0 = inf, spatial part finite, no NaN.
    • Hyperboloid.dist/dist_0 slots 2 and 3 (VERSION_LEGACY, VERSION_LEGACY_SMOOTHENED) are gone; a version_idx outside {0, 1} now raises ValueError. The explicit check is needed because lax.switch silently clamps an out-of-range index into range, so a stale version_idx=2 would have quietly run the smoothened arm instead of failing. The two slots were added in 1.1.2 / 1.2.0 by the very fixes that made them obsolete (the cancellation-free dist rewrite and the origin-chart dist_0 rewrite); at 1.1.1 the switch had only two slots, so no pre-fix code ever passed 2 or 3, and pre-fix numbers remain reproducible by installing hyperbolix==1.1.1 from PyPI. For the record, the four golden values the deleted regression test pinned — on-sheet point at spatial radius 1e-3, c = 1, dim 8, direction e1, true geodesic radius 9.99999833e-4 — were: slot 2 float32 0.0015440807910636067, slot 2 float64 0.0009999998333921221, slot 3 float64 0.16632065504083338, slot 3 float32 0.16632071137428284 (CPU; ±1 ulp on an A100)

    Fixed¶

    • Every zero-initialised hyperboloid gyro-bias was frozen at its init value: ∂(x ⊕ b)/∂b at b = origin was exactly the zero matrix, in both dtypes. dist_0 short-circuited on a bitwise at_origin guard (jnp.where(at_origin, 0.0, res)); logmap_0 scales the spatial part by dist_0(y)/‖y_s‖ and inherited it; Lorentz gyro addition x ⊕ y = Exp_x(PT_{0→x}(Log_0(y))) inherited it in turn. A bias parameter that starts at the origin, which is what use_gyro_bias=True does, therefore received an exactly-zero gradient at step 0 and never left. Affected layers: HypLinearHyperboloidPLFC, HypConv2DHyperboloidILNN, HypLinearHyperboloidBusemann. Measured gyro-bias gradient L2 at init (c = 1, dim 8, batch 4, dist_0 loss, before → after): float32 0.0 → 1.37 (PLFC), 0.0 → 4.24 (ILNN conv), 0.0 → 3.07 (Busemann FC); float64 0.0 → 1.48, 0.0 → 5.37, 0.0 → 1.83. In float32 the gradient was additionally ~1400x over-scaled the moment the bias left the origin, because the acosh floor below was divided by the true radius: the PLFC gradient at bias radius 1e-6 was 1535x its value at 1e-2, a ratio that is now 1.06x. The Poincaré HypLinearPoincareBusemann gyro-bias goes through Möbius addition and was never affected (7.64 before and after). A model trained with a gyro-bias on any of the three hyperboloid layers was trained with that bias pinned to the origin; retraining under this fix changes results.

    • Hyperboloid dist_0/logmap_0 recovered the geodesic radius from the time coordinate x₀, which cannot resolve a small radius; they now read it off the spatial part. dist_0 was acosh(clip(√c·x₀, 1))/√c. On the sheet x₀ = cosh(√c·d)/√c ≈ (1 + c·d²/2)/√c, so d survives only to sqrt(eps) resolution (relative error eps/(2·c·d²)), and acosh's 1 + 10·eps domain clamp flattened every float32 radius below sqrt(20·eps)/√c = 1.5441e-3 onto that floor value (1.5441e-3 was returned for every such point, 1500x too large at radius 1e-6; only the bitwise origin returned 0). The smoothened arm was worse: smooth_clamp_min (β = 50) adds ln2/50 to the acosh argument, i.e. a floor of acosh(1 + ln2/50)/√c = 0.16632/√c under every point that is not bitwise the origin, in both dtypes (measured: it returned 0.16632 at every radius ≤ 1e-2 and 0.1815 at radius 0.1, agreeing with the true value only from radius ≈ 1). Slot 0 is now the exact on-sheet identity arcsinh(√c·‖x_s‖)/√c, with no domain clamp and a derivative bounded by 1; slot 1 is the same with the spatial radius floored in quadrature at 20·eps, a floor of arcsinh(20·eps)/√c (2.4e-6/√c float32, 4.4e-15/√c float64) that equals the pairwise smoothened floor 2·arcsinh(10·eps)/√c from 1.1.2 to first order. logmap_0 is now [0, arcsinh(u)/u · y_s] with u = √c‖y_s‖: it no longer routes through dist_0's version switch, ‖log_0(y)‖ = dist_0(y) holds by construction instead of by cancelling a floored numerator against an unfloored denominator, and the trailing tangent_proj is dropped (it is the identity at the origin, but it routes through the Minkowski inner product, which turns one inf spatial coordinate into an all-NaN vector). scalar_mul is now literally exp_0(r · log_0(x)); its old normalize-then-rescale detour carried a sqrt(max(‖v‖², MIN_NORM)) floor, an effective 3.16e-8 floor on ‖log_0 x‖ that was invisible only while the larger acosh floor sat in front of it.     • On an Ampere or Hopper GPU, lorentz_midpoint and the three hyperbolic attention layers silently ran their matmuls in TF32, costing ~2000x accuracy in the one place the library cannot afford it. XLA:GPU defaults float32 matmuls to TF32, whose 10-bit mantissa carries ~1e-3 relative error against float32's ~1e-7. lorentz_midpoint's whole design is a cancellation-free identity in which every rounded term is O(‖delta‖²), so that the normalizer stays accurate at any radius — TF32 is far coarser than the effect that identity exists to remove. Measured float32-vs-float64 relative error of lorentz_midpoint (A100, jax 0.9.1, c ∈ {0.1, 1.0} × ‖s‖ ∈ {0.5, 5, 20}): GPU default 4.6e-5 … 2.6e-4, GPU with the fix 2.6e-8 … 1.6e-7, CPU 2.6e-8 … 1.2e-7 — i.e. three of the seven significant digits were gone, with no warning, in every hyperbolic attention or Fréchet-mean computation on such a card. The worst site is HyperbolicFullAttention's Lorentzian similarity, which evaluates ⟨Q,K⟩_L = -Q₀K₀ + ⟨Q_s,K_s⟩ as the difference of two matmuls — the exact cancellation _polar_frame was written to avoid, with both halves produced by TF32 GEMMs, so the subtraction amplifies a ~1e-3 input error rather than a ~1e-7 one. Fixed by annotating the cancellation-sensitive dots with precision=jax.lax.Precision.HIGHEST through one documented constant, hyperbolix.nn_layers.hyperboloid_core.MATMUL_PRECISION: the three jnp.einsums in lorentz_midpoint, the ten score / Lorentz-inner-product / aggregate einsums in hyperboloid_attention.py, the spatial residual projection the linear and softmax variants add to that aggregate, and HTCLinear's matmul — the last because it produces the Q/K/V the Lorentz inner product then cancels, and a TF32 input makes an accurate cancellation pointless (annotating only the attention einsums left 6 of the 7 attention tests red; adding HTCLinear left 2; adding the residual projection cleared them). This turned 14 of the 21 GPU-only test failures green (all 7 attention, all 7 lorentz_midpoint). HIGHEST is a no-op on CPU (no TF32 path) and for float64 anywhere; the CPU outputs of lorentz_midpoint and of a HyperbolicFullAttention forward are bit-identical before and after, eager and jitted, in both dtypes. On a tensor-core GPU it does trade GEMM throughput for accuracy, since HIGHEST replaces one TF32 pass with a three-pass float32 emulation: a jitted HyperbolicFullAttention forward at B = 8, N = 256, 4 heads, head dim 64, float32 measured a median 1.84 ms with the fix vs 1.74 ms at the TF32 default, +5.5 % (A100-PCIE-40GB, 5 warmup + 20 timed calls; the card was idle but shared, so treat the ratio as indicative)

    • The ‖z‖ floor in the hyperboloid MLR silently zeroed the entire ∂‖z‖ branch of its gradient on GPU, for one output channel at a time, in roughly two of every three process launches. _compute_mlr floored the hyperplane-normal norm with jnp.linalg.norm(z, ...).clip(min=min_enorm), i.e. jnp.maximum(n, eps). That op's JVP is jax's tie-breaking _balanced_eq, g · [n == ans] / (1 + [eps == ans]) — it tests the operand n for bit equality with the result ans, which is sound only while the two are literally the same value in the compiled graph. XLA:GPU emits n = sqrt(sum(z*z)) twice in the compiled backward — once as its own small fusion, once recomputed inside the fusion that evaluates the ∂‖z‖ branch — and its per-process fusion autotuner picks the emitter for the first one by measured runtime, NATIVE_EMITTER or BLOCK_LEVEL_EMITTER (Triton). The two emitters disagree in the last bit of n for some rows; when they do, [n == ans] is false for that row, the derivative factor becomes 0, and the gradient returned for that output channel is exactly the one you would get by treating ‖z‖ as a constant. Measured on an A100 (jax 0.9.1, linear_plfc layer-contract inputs, float32 vs a float64 reference): jitted gradient relative error 1.0e-2 → 1.5e-7, and in a failing launch the affected row matched the ‖z‖-frozen gradient to 2.5e-8 while every other row was correct to 1.5e-7 — the branch was dropped, not perturbed. The reduced repro failed in 5 of 8 launches before and passed 7 of 7 after; test_gradient_contract[linear_plfc-f32] failed in 3 of 4 pytest launches before and passed 3 of 3 after. This was not conditioning: perturbing ‖z‖ by one float32 ulp in float64 — coherently, or independently at each of its three occurrences in the formula — moves the true gradient by at most 2.4e-7, five orders of magnitude short of the observed error. The floor is now jnp.where(n > min_enorm, n, min_enorm), which compares n against the constant (0.027 vs 1e-15 at these inputs) instead of against a differently-rounded copy of itself, so no 1-ulp disagreement can flip it. Forward values and the CPU gradient are bit-identical before and after in both dtypes, eager and jitted; only a NaN z behaves differently (maximum propagates the NaN, where floors it), which is already outside the function's contract. Flooring the squared sum instead (sqrt(maximum(Σz², eps²))) does not help — it keeps the same maximum tie-break and still failed 4 of 6 launches. The three XLA flags that hid the symptom (--xla_gpu_enable_cublaslt=false, --xla_gpu_autotune_level=0, --xla_gpu_enable_triton_gemm=false) each did so only by removing the emitter choice, not by fixing anything. The same hazard applies to every jnp.maximum(f(x), floor) on a differentiated path — the library has ~30 such MIN_NORM floors — but only this one has been observed to fire; a pure-JAX repro and a draft upstream report are in logs/2026-09-03_plfc_jit_grad/upstream/
    • The same GPU gradient-zeroing hazard is now closed at every other constant floor and clamp in the library — 76 sites. The mechanism is the one in the entry above: jnp.maximum(f(x), CONST)'s JVP is jax's _balanced_eq, which tests the operand for bit equality with the result, and XLA:GPU may compute the operand twice with emitters chosen per process by its fusion autotuner. When the two copies disagree by 1 ulp the whole gradient branch through the operand is silently zeroed. Only the MLR site had been observed to fire, but every constant floor on a differentiated path is exposed, so all of them are rewritten as a comparison against the constant, through three new one-line helpers in hyperbolix.utils.math_utils: floor_at(x, lo) = where(x < lo, lo, x), cap_at(x, hi) = where(x > hi, hi, x), and clamp_to(x, lo, hi) = cap_at(floor_at(x, lo), hi). The comparison direction is chosen so a NaN operand still propagates exactly as maximum/minimum/clip propagated it (NaN < lo is false, so the NaN is selected), which makes the forward value identical for every input — finite, NaN, and ±inf — at all 76 sites, in both dtypes, eager and jitted. All three clamps (smooth_clamp, smooth_clamp_min, smooth_clamp_max) are re-derived as gate-free closed forms: the two-sided clamp is the difference form min + softplus_β(x−min) − softplus_β(x−max), provably inside (min, max) for every window and β — composing the two one-sided clamps (the old construction) overshoots a narrow window by up to ln(1+e^(−βw))/β, which for βw ≲ 1 is the size of the window itself; a test now pins that the composition fails so it can't be "simplified" back. Evaluation uses the hinge+remainder split clip(x) + tail − tail (tails ≤ ln 2/β), avoiding float32 catastrophic cancellation, and the gradient is exactly sigmoid(β(x−min)) − sigmoid(β(x−max)) with correct one-sided values at the hinge. Downstream consequence: the VERSION_SMOOTHENED hyperboloid distance is now monotone with floor acosh(1+ln2/β)/√c ≈ 0.166/√c and agrees with VERSION_DEFAULT beyond ≈ 0.8/√c (it was previously non-monotone near coincident points)
    • Euclidean.is_in_manifold returned constant True, accepting NaN/Inf — it now checks finiteness. The same defect existed on is_in_tangent_space for Euclidean, Poincare, and Stereographic (the tangent space of an open subset of R^n is R^n — finiteness is the only constraint); all three fixed
    • Wrapped-normal log-det-Jacobian Taylor gate is now dtype-aware. Validated against a high-precision oracle on both sides of both gates
    • LearnableCurvature's straight-through clamp amplified gradients in the clamped region. With parameterization="log" at the exponent cap, the pass-through handed the raw parameter a gradient scaled by dc/draw = c — up to e^15 at the cap — so one step could catapult the parameter across its whole range. The pass-through now divides the clamped-region gradient by max(dc/draw, 1) (implemented as a stop-gradient carrier swap, so no reciprocal is formed that would flush to zero in float32 at the cap). Interior gradients are bit-identical, the forward value is unchanged, and gradients below the lower clamp are never amplified either
    • ProperVelocity.dist/dist_0 returned NaN gradients at the origin (grad dist(0,0), grad dist_0(0)): bare jnp.linalg.norm has a 0/0 VJP at the zero vector.     Added¶

    • CO-SNE (hyperbolix.decomposition.CoSNE) — dimensionality reduction and visualization for hyperbolic data (Guo et al. 2022), the hyperbolic analogue of t-SNE. A thin sklearn-style class (fit / fit_transform; non-parametric, so no out-of-sample transform, matching sklearn's TSNE) over a pure, JIT-friendly functional core (fit_cosne, conditional_probabilities, joint_probabilities, low_dim_probabilities, kl_divergence_loss, magnitude_loss). High-dimensional similarities are perplexity-calibrated on squared hyperbolic distances (paper Eq. 2); low-dimensional similarities use a heavy-tailed Student-t / Cauchy kernel on Poincaré-ball distances (paper Eq. 9 — both the author's-code plain-distance form, default, and the exact squared-distance form via exact_cauchy=True). On top of the t-SNE KL divergence it adds a magnitude loss H = (1/N)·Σᵢ(‖xᵢ‖² − ‖yᵢ‖²)² (paper Eq. 10) that preserves each point's distance-to-origin (the hierarchy depth). Optimization is two-stage projected gradient descent on the ball (early-exaggeration KL-only exploration, then KL + magnitude), with the KL gradient Riemannian-scaled by (1 − c‖y‖²)²/4 and the magnitude gradient Euclidean (paper §3.7). Curvature-general (a single c for both spaces, passed at fit time); Poincaré input (N, D) → (N, K) ball coordinates, Hyperboloid input (N, A) → (N, K+1). Uses exact autodiff gradients of the declared losses — a deliberate deviation from the reference's demonstrably buggy hand gradients — so the reference learning rates do not transfer; the learning_rate default (0.5) is calibrated on a synthetic-cluster recovery test and documented on the class. Double precision recommended for fitting
    • HoroPCA (hyperbolix.decomposition.HoroPCA) — hyperbolic dimensionality reduction via horospherical projections (Chami et al. 2021). A thin sklearn-style class (fit / transform / fit_transform) over a pure, independently usable and JIT-friendly functional core (fit_horopca, transform_horopca, horo_projection, horopca_loss). All computation runs on the hyperboloid (Poincaré input is mapped in via the exact isometry); K ideal points are jointly optimized with Adam + gradient clipping to maximize the pairwise-distance variance of the horospherically projected data, which preserves every Busemann coordinate. Poincaré input (N, D) → (N, K) ball coordinates; Hyperboloid input (N, A) → (N, K+1). K = 1 is supported via a closed form (the reference's Sherman–Morrison inverse is singular there). Exposes components_, mean_, boost_, losses_, and pairwise-variance-based explained_variance_ / total_variance_ / explained_variance_ratio_. Double precision recommended for fitting
    • hyperbolix.decomposition.frechet_mean — manifold-generic Karcher (Fréchet) mean via fixed-point iteration in a lax.while_loop (Lorentz-centroid init on the hyperboloid, projected-mean init on the Poincaré ball). The data-centering primitive HoroPCA builds on; configurable step_size / tol / max_iters

    Added¶

    • LearnableCurvature(nnx.Module) — canonical module for trainable curvature in hyperbolix.utils.curvature, also exported at top level (from hyperbolix import LearnableCurvature). Introduces the softplus (default, c = softplus(raw), bounded gradient via sigmoid, van Spengler 2023 convention) and log (c = exp(raw), scale-invariant gradient dc/draw = c, MERU convention, preferred for compiled RL loops) reparameterizations. The default clamp is applied to the recovered c (not the raw parameter) as a hard stability guard for long compiled training loops — [0.1, 10.0] for both schemes; pass c_min=None, c_max=None to disable. Updated by any standard Euclidean nnx.Optimizer — no Riemannian optimizer needed

    Changed¶

    • Manifolds are now plain Python classes (not nnx.Module). This structurally prevents shared-manifold bugs in nnx.scan/nnx.fori_loop — manifolds become static graphdef attributes with no state pytree entries
    • learnable=True removed from manifold constructors. Use the LearnableCurvature module instead — assign one instance per distinct curvature on your model. See the Manifolds User Guide
    • ProductManifold is now a plain class; from_signature accepts 3- and 4-tuple specs only (5-tuple learnable override removed)
    • learnable_curvature() / get_curvature() functional helpers replaced by the LearnableCurvature(nnx.Module) class. The class bundles the raw parameter, reparameterization scheme, and clamp bounds in one object, making accidental init/recovery mismatches structurally impossible. Call sites change from c = get_curvature(self.c_raw) to c = self.curvature(). Breaking change — no deprecation shim
